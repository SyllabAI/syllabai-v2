# ADR-044: The Editor Role and the Content Workbench — Human Edits to the Corpus of Record Through the Gate Pipeline

- **Status:** PROPOSED — filed with the operator order "Re-file the spec where it counts" (trace 1a1bf9b23f1d45a9, 2026-11-09). Becomes **ACCEPTED on merge** (house law, ADR-040/041 precedent).
- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Related:** `packages/contracts/src/auth.ts` (the frozen-core Role vocabulary of record) · `packages/contracts/src/content-writes.ts` (the frozen teacher-ingestion band — a different plane, see §4) · `apps/api/src/middleware/auth.ts` (the requireAuth/requireRole shell law) · `apps/hub/src/lib/identity.ts` (the derived identity surface) · ADR-039 (disclosed data edits) · the RN-DEBRIS-11 EDX-2 corruption class (the founding lesson) · the census-guard CI check (the last wall)
- **Supersedes:** none. **Amends:** none.
- **Companion evidence:** the reference Workbench MVP (built and browser-verified in the originating session: API golden path 20/20, UI flow 10/10, lint clean) and its ADR draft; both lived in recycled sandbox storage and are superseded by this filing as the design of record.

## 1. Context

Today, every byte of the corpus of record — the question banks, revision notes, spines, and flashcard decks that the hub serves — is written by machine lanes under the full discipline this house has built: preregistered runs, receipts, byte-asserted worklog appends, the prebuild gate chain, and the census-guard CI check that fails closed on any serving-plane drift. Human beings have no write path at all, and that is by design: the EDX-2 corruption (RN-DEBRIS-11) happened precisely because content mutations occurred downstream of the corpus of record through unaudited paths — gate-invisible, per-field, detectable only by cross-field census. The lesson of record is architectural, not cosmetic: unguarded mutation anywhere below the corpus is how the serving plane rots.

The operator has now ruled that humans get a sanctioned write path: a new user type — named **editor** — that can edit the official content the site renders, including the Official-ExamQuestions banks and revision notes. The gap this ADR closes is precise. The frozen-core identity port pins `Role.java` to exactly `{ STUDENT, TEACHER, ADMIN }`, and `UserView.roles` can only ever contain those three names (three construction sites, all through `UserView.from` — pinned in `packages/contracts/src/auth.ts`). The hub's derived identity (`apps/hub/src/lib/identity.ts`) maps TEACHER/ADMIN to the "teacher" workspace role and everything else to "student". Meanwhile the existing write-flow contracts (`content-writes.ts`) are the frozen teacher-ingestion band — draft → validate → place pipelines for *new* content into the DB — not human curation of the served corpus. Nothing today gives a trusted human a governed way to fix a typo in a served question stem, and nothing today would let them do it without becoming a new EDX-2.

The operator's rulings are on record and bind this design: **Tier 1 auto-merge is acceptable**; **the editor's scope covers all banks at once and future ones** (no per-bank entitlement list, no physics-first policy); **the role is named editor**; **holders may be internal or external**. This ADR turns those rulings into an implementation-level design of record on the house stack — Drizzle, the two-envelope validation law, the F5-S7 grants law, the receipts law — so the next session builds one product instead of rediscovering the argument.

## 2. The One Law (E-0)

**The serving plane is never directly editable.** Every human edit flows corpus → gates → tiered merge → receipt → census → serve, in that order, with no shortcuts. The hub's `apps/hub/content/**` tree is a derived artifact rebuilt by the import stack from the corpus of record (`syllabai-resources`); an editor who writes to it directly would be an unguarded post-build stack of flesh — the exact failure class this house just finished eradicating. Concretely, E-0 forbids: direct writes to serving-plane JSON from any editor surface; free-text AI rewrite passes over banks (a post-build mutation with no field grammar — EDX-2 again); edits that bypass the PR pipeline; and per-field locking (premature complexity — the tier classifier plus git history already arbitrate conflicts). The Workbench may *render* served artifacts for context, but every mutation targets the corpus of record through a proposal, and nothing user-visible changes until census-guard has countersigned the rebuilt plane. If E-0 ever becomes inconvenient, the correct response is to amend this ADR in public, not to route around it in code.

## 3. Role Identity: the V2-Native Editor Grant

The first implementation decision is where "editor" lives, and the frozen-core parity law decides it. Adding a fourth member to the auth `Role` vocabulary would force a divergence into the capture/verify band that pins the core's identity surfaces — every golden capture of `UserView` would need re-adjudication, for a capability the core never modeled. This house already has the cheaper, honest pattern: **v2-native additive planes with no frozen-core counterpart** (the workspace-docs class — `user_documents` shipped as a pure additive table and router with no capture band). The editor role takes that path.

Editor-ness is a **grant row, not an enum member**. A new `editor_grants` table (§7) holds `(user_id, scopes, granted_by, granted_at, revoked_at)`; possession of an unrevoked row *is* the role. The consequences fall out cleanly. The frozen `UserView.roles` stays exactly `{ STUDENT, TEACHER, ADMIN }` — zero divergence, zero re-capture. Composability is natural: a person can be TEACHER and hold an editor grant, and the grant confers **zero platform powers** — no user management, no classes, no role granting (that remains with whoever administers roles today; the bootstrap-admin surface is untouched). Revocation writes `revoked_at`; because the API checks the grant at request time rather than embedding it in long-lived JWTs, revocation is effective on the next request without a token-version bump. External holders need nothing but a hub account: the Workbench abstracts git entirely (§6), and internal editors who prefer raw git may file PRs by hand — the pipeline treats both identically.

Enforcement mirrors the existing shell law exactly. A `requireEditor` middleware composes after `requireAuth`: unauthenticated requests take the Boot-style 401 body before anything else runs; authenticated students (and grant-less teachers) take the 403 body; grant holders proceed. No new error shapes, no new auth plumbing — the teacher-me class verbatim, with a different predicate.

## 4. Relationship to Existing Write Planes

This ADR adds a plane; it does not reinterpret the two that exist. The **frozen teacher-ingestion band** (`content-writes.ts`: past-paper drafts, curriculum drafts, validate/reject/place flows) remains the machine-parity pipeline for ingesting *new* content into the DB — it is Jackson-mirrored, capture-pinned, and untouched here. The **lane pipeline** (prereg → payload → receipt → worklog → PR → gates) remains the of-record path for *bulk* and *structural* corpus surgery — the RN-DEBRIS-11 run-012 spine repair is the canonical specimen, and no human workbench should imitate it ad hoc. The **editor plane** is the narrow middle: single-question, single-note, field-scoped human curation, volume-grade and gate-guarded, arriving as PRs that the same CI everybody else passes must also pass. Editors get no new standing in the other two planes, and the other two planes gain no editor bypass. If a proposed edit is really structural work wearing a typo's clothing, the tier classifier (§6) routes it to Tier 3 and the lane discipline takes over — the planes hand off by classification, never by preference.

## 5. Capability Matrix

The matrix of record, enforceable at three layers (middleware on the API, route-gating in the hub, and the Workbench's own view switching):

| Capability | student | teacher | editor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read served content | yes | yes | yes |
| Classes / assignments / grading | — | yes | — |
| Propose content edits (Workbench) | — | — | yes |
| Merge to corpus of record | — | — | tiered (§6): Tier 1 auto on green; Tier 2 four-eyes; Tier 3 lane discipline |
| User management / role grants | — | — | — |

Two readings keep this honest. First, the editor column is strictly content-scoped: a grant is not a promotion — it carries no entitlements outside the corpus pipeline, and the matrix's empty cells are enforced refusals, not missing features. Second, the "merge" row is deliberately not a flat yes: the editor's merge power is *conditional on tier and gates*, which is what makes a Tier 1 auto-merge ruling safe to hold. The hub surfaces this additively: `identity.ts` keeps its two-value workspace `role` untouched (the frozen mapping stands), and an additive `capabilities` field on the derived identity snapshot (populated from a v2-native `GET /api/v1/editor/me`, cached with the same store pattern the teacher sync uses) gates Workbench visibility. Students and signed-out sessions never contact the editor plane — the identity-gated law the teacher stores already established.

## 6. The Edit Pipeline and the Tier Classifier

Every edit is a **proposal**, and the pipeline is the same seven steps regardless of tier. (1) *Render*: the Workbench shows the served artifact exactly as served — never raw JSON — and toggles to structured edit mode over canonical fields (stem, choices, answer, solution, per-part). (2) *Pre-flight*: the candidate delta runs the gate engine (§8) client-side as inline validation, so editors see refusals before they become CI failures. (3) *Propose*: the API classifies the tier server-side (the client's classification is advisory, never authoritative), persists the proposal, and — for tiers whose merge path is automated — opens a PR on `syllabai-resources` with a structured description: who, what, why, the field-level diff, and the gate-run receipt. (4) *Gate*: CI runs the corpus gates as it does for every lane PR. (5) *Merge by tier*: Tier 1 auto-merges on green (the operator's ruling); Tier 2 requires a second, distinct human approver through the review queue before the auto-merge fires; Tier 3 is closed from the Workbench entirely and handed to the lane process with a prereg requirement. (6) *Receipt*: every state transition writes a receipt row — actor, field, before/after hash, gate run id, PR url — and the proposal's journal renders in the hub. (7) *Serve*: after merge, the import stack rebuilds the plane bank; the rebuilt bank lands only behind a census-guard exit-0 receipt, the standing law; the serve step itself is lane-only and invisible to editors.

The **tier classifier** is what makes unequal edits unequal, and its classes are fixed here so the Workbench cannot improvise them. **Tier 1 — typography and formatting**: KaTeX spacing, part labels, markup hygiene, punctuation — the T-OEQ-PHYS-FORMAT-1 domain. High volume, low risk, auto-merge on green. **Tier 2 — content-bearing**: answer keys, solution semantics, choice text, notes prose that changes meaning. Four-eyes is mandatory: the author cannot approve their own proposal (enforced server-side by G-EYES), and a post-merge census receipt is mandatory before serve. **Tier 3 — structural**: adding or removing questions or parts, moving sets across topics, bank reorganization. The Workbench refuses to carry Tier 3: it discloses the classification and stops, because structural surgery without preregistration is how run-012-sized changes lose their evidence trail. The classifier's boundary tests are part of the gate engine's own test suite, so a misclassification is a bug with a failing test, not a judgment call.

## 7. Data Plane of Record

Two tables, one additive Drizzle migration — **`packages/db/drizzle/0028_editor-planes.sql`** (0027 is the latest of record) — plus the standard meta snapshots and rebaseline pins, exactly the 0023/0026 pattern. `editor_edit_proposals`: identity (uuid pk), `user_id` (fk users, onDelete cascade — the 0022/0023 personal-state law), `course_slug` (kebab CHECK — server-side re-verified as defense-in-depth), `bank` (closed vocabulary: questions / notes / flashcards), `target_path` (corpus-relative), `tier` (1/2/3, server-classified), `fields` (jsonb: the field-level before/after delta), `base_sha` (the corpus commit the edit was cut against), `proposal_pr` (nullable until the PR exists), `state` (draft / preflight_failed / queued_review / approved / merged / rejected / superseded), timestamps. `editor_edit_receipts`: proposal fk, actor, action (propose / preflight_refusal / approve / reject / stale_regate / merge / serve), `before_hash`, `after_hash`, `gate_run_id`, `detail` (jsonb), created_at — append-only by application law, the P1 receipt pattern extended from scripts to humans.

The tables are dead weight without the F5-S7 grants law: the app role (`syllabai_v2_app_w2r2`) holds no DML on new tables until an owner-capable grants lane runs — the teach_sessions 500 class proved that lesson at production cost. This ADR therefore cards the companion grants run on the `t-tier1-apply` template verbatim (preflight census fail-closed, ownership gate, one GRANT transaction, `has_table_privilege` wire proof as the receipt) as a **Phase 1 exit criterion**, not an afterthought. Caps follow the T-CAPS-POLICY-1 style and are pinned in the contracts module: open proposals per user (10), receipt rows per proposal (200), field delta bytes per proposal (512KB — the WORKSPACE_DOC_MAX_BYTES figure, reused because it is already load-tested). The contracts module (`packages/contracts/src/editor.ts`) is v2-native additive with no frozen-core counterpart: request schemas two-envelope-shaped (the shell classifies `malformed_body` before `validation_failed`), response schemas mirror the records, and every cap is a pinned test.

## 8. API Surface and the Gate Engine

The v2-native router (`/api/v1/editor/*`, mounted additively — the teacher-me class) is small by design: `GET /me` — the capability surface (200 with scopes for grant holders, 403 otherwise; the identity-gated client law means no one else ever calls it); `GET /banks` — the corpus inventory derived from the resources HEAD, read-only; `POST /preflight` — the gate engine run over a candidate delta, returning the typed refusal list without persisting anything; `POST /proposals` — create (server-side tier classification; auto-merge path only for Tier 1 with all gates green and a clean base_sha); `GET /proposals` and `GET /proposals/:id` — the queue and detail; `POST /proposals/:id/review` — approve/reject for Tier 2 (G-EYES enforced: author id ≠ approver id, 403 otherwise; a stale `base_sha` forces a re-gate before an approval can bind); `GET /proposals/:id/receipts` — the audit journal. There is deliberately **no** editor-reachable serve or merge-to-main endpoint: the pipeline's step 7 belongs to the lane machinery alone.

The **gate engine** is the heart, and its whole value is *singleness*: one module, consumed twice. The same law implementations run (a) in the hub bundle for client-side pre-flight and (b) in the CI/prebuild context as the corpus gates. The law families of record, each a typed refusal with a stable code an editor can read: **Canon (G-CANON)** — content canon at level 3, the rendering-of-record rules for markdown/KaTeX field content; **Serialization (G-SER)** — the serialization law of the corpus files (indent = 1, no trailing newline, key order of record); **Field grammar (G-FIELD)** — per-field shape laws (choice arity, part structure, code formats); **Character hygiene (G-CHAR)** — the PUA/control-character ban; **Asset guard (G-ASSET)** — asset-byte references must resolve (the dangling-image class the site-wide crawl caught); **Author independence (G-EYES)** — the four-eyes predicate; **Scope (G-SCOPE)** — tier bounds (a Tier 3-shaped delta offered at Tier 1 is refused, not reclassified). The reference MVP implemented ten typed refusals across these families and its fixtures (including the three wave-D-repaired physics questions as regression pins) carry over as the engine's test corpus. Parity between the two consumers is enforced by making the engine a single shared package — a gate that exists only in the UI is a lie, and a gate that exists only in CI is a bad surprise; both directions are the EDX-2 shape in miniature.

## 9. The Workbench in the Hub

The Workbench is a hub surface, reachable only with the editor capability, and its design debt is deliberately small because the reference MVP already validated the interaction model end to end. The surfaces: **bank browser** — courses and banks with served-state counts, read-only, derived from `GET /banks`; **question view** — the served rendering (the exact production components, not a re-implementation) with a toggle into structured edit mode over canonical fields; **pre-flight panel** — the typed gate refusals inline, each with its code, the offending field, and the smallest corrective hint; **proposal journal** — the receipts for the user's own proposals (state, tier, PR link, hashes); **review queue** — for Tier 2 approvers, with the diff, the gate state, and the two actions G-EYES governs. The MVP's verified golden path is the acceptance script for Phase 2: act as an editor, open a bank, repair a field, watch pre-flight type the gates, propose, see Tier 1 auto-merge with a receipt; then a content-bearing edit lands in the four-eyes queue where its author is refused as their own approver. One deliberate MVP disclosure carries into the build: the reference implementation had **no networked commit path** (snapshot fixtures, disclaimed in-app) — Phase 3 is where the GitHub App installation replaces the simulated PR with a real one on `syllabai-resources`, and not one line of the pipeline above changes shape when it does.

## 10. Phasing

**Phase 0 — this filing** (done at merge): the ADR of record + receipt; zero code bytes. **Phase 1 — role plumbing and history**: `0028_editor-planes.sql` + schema/meta rebaseline; the grants lane (exit criterion); `requireEditor`; `GET /me`; the additive `capabilities` field in the hub identity; and the read-only per-question edit-history view — nearly free, because the corpus is git: surface commit/blame for the bank path in the hub and editors gain provenance before they gain pens. **Phase 2 — Workbench MVP, Tier 1 only**: the gate engine as a shared package with its test corpus, pre-flight, proposals with auto-merge, the journal; verified against the MVP's golden path (API golden path and browser flow both green, as the reference run demonstrated 20/20 and 10/10). **Phase 3 — full tiers, real wiring**: GitHub App PR path on `syllabai-resources`, the Tier 2 review queue live, census-guard-coupled serve, Tier 3 disclosed hand-off to lanes. **Phase 4 — feedback**: edit volume and refusal-class metrics into the lane process (the same receipts-as-data habit the worklog already runs on). Each phase is independently shippable and independently revertible; no phase holds a previous phase hostage.

## 11. What Is Not Built

The negative space is part of the design. No free-text AI rewrite passes over banks — a mutation with no field grammar, no receipt granularity, and no gate vocabulary is EDX-2 with better manners. No direct serving-plane editing from any surface, ever. No edits without PRs — the PR *is* the audit boundary, the receipt is the audit trail, and a pipeline that can skip either is not the pipeline this ADR describes. No per-field locking — base_sha plus tier arbitration is enough, and real contention is a measured problem before it is an engineered one. No editor self-merge above Tier 1, no editor access to user management or role grants, and no editor-reachable serve trigger. Nothing here changes the frozen-core identity surfaces, the teacher-ingestion band, or the lane pipeline; three planes, three mandates, zero new couplings.

## 12. Consequences and Risks

The wins are structural: humans finally get a governed write path, gate refusals move from CI-time (minutes, cryptic) to edit-time (seconds, typed), every human edit acquires a receipt trail with the same rigor lanes have, and the EDX-2 class loses its only remaining entry point — well-meaning people with no pipeline. The risks are named and owned. **Gate divergence** (UI gates drifting from CI gates) is bounded by the single-package engine and its cross-consumer tests; a divergence bug is a P1 against this ADR, not a patch note. **Grants-lag** (tables live, app role ungranted) is the known production-killer; Phase 1's exit criterion closes it. **Classifier abuse** (cosmetic wrappers around content-bearing deltas) is bounded by G-SCOPE's boundary tests and by the four-eyes default for anything the classifier cannot pin to Tier 1 with a test. **External-holder trust** is bounded by the grant row's revocability and the receipts journal's completeness — an editor's blast radius is exactly their proposals, and every one is attributable. The census-guard remains the last wall throughout: even a hypothetical pipeline failure cannot reach a learner without an exit-0 census receipt, and that wall stays standing no matter what this ADR ships.
